# Presentation standards

A slide deck supports a speaker, or stands in for one when it is sent ahead. Either way each
slide makes one point and shows the evidence for it.

## Structure

- **Title slide**: the title, who it is for, the presenter, the date.
- **Agenda** for any deck longer than about eight slides.
- **Sections**, each opened by a divider slide that says what the section will show.
- **Close with the ask**: the decision or next steps you want from the audience.

## Deck shapes

Most decks follow one of these orders. Pick the one that matches the purpose, then cut
what the audience does not need.

| Purpose | Order of sections |
| :-- | :-- |
| Pitch or proposal | problem, why it matters now, solution, how it works, market, traction, business model, competition, roadmap, team, the ask |
| Business or data review | executive summary with the three numbers that matter, results against target, what drove them, risks, decisions needed |
| Product launch | the customer's problem, the product, three features that matter, how it looks (screens), availability and pricing, next steps |
| Strategy or plan | where we are, where we want to be, the choices, the plan by phase, how progress is measured, what we need |
| Technical update | what shipped, what changed and why, status by workstream, issues and risks, next milestones |

Every shape ends the same way: the decision or the next step you want, then a closing slide.

## The headline states the point

Use the **assertion-evidence** approach (Michael Alley's research on engineering and
scientific presentations): the slide title is a full sentence that states the point,
and the body is the evidence for it, preferably a picture, chart or table rather than bullets.

| Weak topic title | Assertion title |
| :-- | :-- |
| Onboarding | Onboarding takes five days today |
| Architecture | One platform replaces three hand-offs |
| Timeline | Live in eight months |

A reader who reads only the titles should get the whole argument.

## Pick the layout from the content

| The content | Slide |
| :-- | :-- |
| One sentence the audience must remember | `statement` |
| Two to four headline numbers | `stats`, or a `kpi` widget when each has a change or a note |
| Three to six parallel ideas, features or modules | `cards` |
| Before and after, us and them, in scope and out | `compare` |
| A sequence | `steps`, or a `timeline` widget with dates |
| A list with a conclusion | `numbered` with a callout |
| A trend, a comparison, a share | a chart widget (see `widgets.md`) |
| Screens of a product | `gallery`, or `media` with the story beside one screen |
| Clients or partners | `logos` |

A deck that alternates slide shapes holds attention; ten slides of bullets in a row lose it.

## Rules

- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs two titles, it is two slides.
- **At most about six bullets**, one line each where possible. The builder warns above the
  theme's limit.
- **Type large enough to read from the back**: body 18 pt or more, nothing below 12 pt except
  footers. Never shrink text to fit; cut or split instead.
- **Numbers in context**: say what a number is compared with ("5 days, against 1 day at
  competitors"), and round to what the audience needs.
- **Charts**: one message per chart, stated in the title; label lines and bars directly
  instead of using a legend where possible.
- **Tables**: few rows, figures right-aligned, the row that matters emphasised.
- **Contrast**: text at least 4.5:1 against its background (WCAG 2.2); never colour alone.
- **Speaker notes** carry what you will say; the slide carries what the audience must see.
- **Plain text**: no emoji or decorative symbols in titles or bullets.
- **One accent, used for meaning.** Neutral greys carry most of the slide; the accent marks
  the one number, bar or step that matters. Section slides may take the full dark or accent
  colour, which makes the break visible.
- **White space is part of the design.** A slide that is full is a slide with two ideas.
- **Big numbers are big**: a headline figure is several times the body size, with a short
  label under it and its comparison beside it.

## Before presenting

Render the PDF and look at every slide: nothing overflowing its box, nothing overlapping the
logo or footer, every picture sharp at full screen, every placeholder filled.
